What They Say → What They Mean

Every variation of the scam/Reddit objection and exactly how to handle it

🟠 "I saw some stuff on Reddit about you guys"

What they mean: "I'm interested but scared. Give me a reason to trust you."
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1: Validate
"I respect that you looked into it. Most people don't do their homework like that. That actually tells me you're the kind of person who'd do well here."
Step 2: Reframe
"Have you ever noticed how on Reddit, the loudest voices are always the people who failed? Nobody who's making money sits on Reddit writing reviews. They're too busy trading."
Step 3: Proof
"I'll be real with you. I've mentored over a thousand students. Not everyone makes it. The ones who fail? They blame the program. The ones who succeed? They're posting gains in my Discord. Same program, different effort."
Step 4: Evidence
"Let me send you something real. Actual P&L screenshots from my students. Not testimonials I wrote. Real numbers."
🔄 FOLLOW-UP
"Look, I show my red days too. I'm not selling a dream. I'm selling a skill. Check my YouTube. I'm transparent about everything."
💥 KILL SHOT
"The question isn't whether some random person on Reddit thinks I'm legit. The question is... are YOU going to keep losing money on your own, or are you going to learn how to do this properly?"
🚫 WHAT NOT TO SAY
Never say "Reddit is fake" or "those people are lying."
Never get defensive. It confirms their suspicion.
Never dismiss their concern. Acknowledge it, then redirect.

📝 "I read some bad reviews"

What they mean: "I need social proof to override the negative"
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1: Curiosity
"Which reviews? I'm curious because I want to know what you saw."
Step 2: Counter with Proof
"I'll tell you something. Anyone can write anything on the internet. What you can't fake is real student results. These are real students, real P&L screenshots, real money."
Step 3: Authority
"I've been public about my trading for years. 1 million followers didn't come from being a scam. But don't take my word for it. Here are students on video sharing their experience."
🔄 FOLLOW-UP
"At the end of the day, you're not paying for a review. You're learning in a skill. Would you rather listen to anonymous strangers or see real results?"

🛡️ "Is this legit?" / "Is this a scam?"

What they mean: "I want this to be real. Convince me."
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1: Flip It
"I love this question because it means you're smart enough to ask. Most people just hand their money to anyone with a nice Instagram."
Step 2: Transparency
"Let me ask you this. Have you watched my YouTube? I show every trade, red and green. I show my losses. Does that sound like a scam to you?"
Step 3: Track Record
"I've been doing this for years. Over a thousand students. If I was a scam, I'd be shut down by now. But here I am, growing every month, trading live every morning."
💥 KILL SHOT
"Look, I get it. The trading education space is full of frauds. That's exactly why KCU exists. To be the real thing. I can't make you trust me over text. But I CAN show you the results."

👥 "A friend told me not to sign up"

What they mean: "Someone who never tried it is projecting their fear onto me"
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1: The Question
"Is your friend a profitable trader?"
Step 2: Reframe
"I respect that your friend cares about you. But has your friend tried KCU? Or are they just repeating what they read online?"
Step 3: Truth Bomb
"Here's the thing. The people who warn you about trading education are usually the ones who've never invested in themselves. They're comfortable being broke."
🔄 FOLLOW-UP
"Tell your friend to check the results page. Then ask them if they can show you those kinds of gains."

Looking for Guaranteed Returns
