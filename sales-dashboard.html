What They Say → What They Mean

Every variation of the scam/Reddit objection and exactly how to handle it

🟠 "I saw some stuff on Reddit about you guys"

What they mean: "I'm interested but scared. Give me a reason to trust you."
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1 — Validate
"I appreciate you bringing that up — most people don't, and it tells me you're doing your homework. That's exactly the kind of person we want in KCU."
Step 2 — Reframe
"Have you ever noticed how on Reddit, the loudest voices are always the people who failed? Nobody who's making money sits on Reddit writing reviews. They're too busy trading."
Step 3 — Proof
"I'll be straight with you — we've mentored over a thousand students. Not everyone makes it. The ones who fail? They blame the program. The ones who succeed? They're posting gains in our Discord. Same program, different effort."
Step 4 — Evidence
"Let me send you something real — actual P&L screenshots from our students. Not testimonials we wrote. Real numbers."
🔄 FOLLOW-UP
"Look, I show my red days too. I'm not selling a dream. I'm selling a skill. Check my YouTube — I'm transparent about everything."
💥 KILL SHOT
"The question isn't whether some random person on Reddit thinks I'm legit. The question is — are YOU going to keep losing money on your own, or are you going to learn how to do this properly?"
🚫 WHAT NOT TO SAY
Never say "Reddit is fake" or "those people are lying."
Never get defensive. It confirms their suspicion.
Never dismiss their concern. Acknowledge it, then redirect.

📝 "I read some bad reviews"

What they mean: "I need social proof to override the negative"
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1 — Curiosity
"Which reviews? I'm curious because I want to know what you saw."
Step 2 — Counter with Proof
"I'll tell you something — anyone can write anything on the internet. What you can't fake is real student results. These are real students, real P&L screenshots, real money."
Step 3 — Authority
"I've been public about my trading for years. 1 million followers didn't come from being a scam. But don't take my word for it — here are students on video sharing their experience."
🔄 FOLLOW-UP
"At the end of the day, you're not investing in a review — you're investing in a skill. Would you rather listen to anonymous strangers or see real results?"

🛡️ "Is this legit?" / "Is this a scam?"

What they mean: "I want this to be real. Convince me."
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1 — Flip It
"I love this question because it means you're smart enough to ask. Most people just hand their money to anyone with a nice Instagram."
Step 2 — Transparency
"Let me ask you this — have you watched my YouTube? I show every trade, red and green. I show my losses. Does that sound like a scam to you?"
Step 3 — Track Record
"We've been operating for years. Over a thousand students. If we were a scam, we'd be shut down by now. But here we are, growing every month."
💥 KILL SHOT
"Look, I get it. The trading education space is full of frauds. That's exactly why KCU exists — to be the real thing. I can't make you trust me over text. But I CAN show you the results."

👥 "A friend told me not to sign up"

What they mean: "Someone who never tried it is projecting their fear onto me"
🎯 THE PLAY
Step 1 — The Question
"Is your friend a profitable trader?"
Step 2 — Reframe
"I respect that your friend cares about you. But has your friend tried KCU? Or are they just repeating what they read online?"
Step 3 — Truth Bomb
"Here's the thing — the people who warn you about trading education are usually the ones who've never invested in themselves. They're comfortable being broke."
🔄 FOLLOW-UP
"Tell your friend to check the results page. Then ask them if they can show you those kinds of gains."

The 10 Commandments of KCU Sales

Thou shalt always go for the close. A conversation without a close attempt is just a chat. Ask for the sale.

🧠 The Mindset

How to think about each call

You're a Doctor, Not a Salesman

You're diagnosing their problem (stuck in a 9-5, inconsistent income, no skills) and prescribing the solution (KCU). You're not selling — you're helping.

You Already Know the Answer

The program works. The results are real. You're not hoping they buy — you're confident it will change their life. Sell from certainty.

Their Objection Is Their Fear Talking

Price, timing, trust — it's all fear dressed up in different clothes. Your job is to help them see past the fear to the opportunity.

Every "No" Is Data

Each rejection tells you something. Were you too generic? Did you miss an objection? Did you not build enough urgency? Learn from every conversation.

You're Betting On Them

"I'm willing to bet on you" isn't just a line — it's the attitude. Genuine belief in their potential is the most powerful sales tool you have.

Urgency Is a Service

Creating urgency isn't manipulation — it's helping them stop procrastinating on their dreams. Every day they wait is a day they lose money.

🚩 Red Flags — When to Walk Away

Not every lead is worth closing. Know when to move on.

Cash Flow = 0.8%

If they genuinely can't afford ANY payment option, they're not ready. Don't push someone into debt. Mark for re-engagement in 3-6 months.

Commitment Issues = 0.8%

If they can't commit to learning, they won't commit to the program. Save your energy for serious leads.

Spouse Says Hard No

Spouse without approval = 0% conversion. If the partner is firmly against it and won't talk, walk away gracefully.

Hostile/Aggressive Tone

If they're combative, insulting, or treating you like a scammer despite proof — they're not your customer. Move on.

Looking for Guaranteed Returns

If they won't accept that trading involves risk and expect guaranteed income, they'll be a liability. Don't oversell.

Multiple Ghosting Cycles

If they've ghosted 3+ times across different follow-up sequences, they're dead. Archive and move on to warm leads.
